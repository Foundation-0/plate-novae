# Scouting notes, 19 October 2026

The research behind plate-novae so far, from Foundation0's
[scouting](https://github.com/Foundation-0/f0/tree/main/deep-fields) of 18–19 October 2026. Facts are split by
whether someone has checked them against their source.

- **Question.** Did any known nova erupt earlier, unseen, on the Harvard plates (about 1890 to 1990)? An earlier
  eruption would make it a recurrent nova.
- **In plain words.** A nova is a white dwarf that suddenly brightens by thousands of times when gas from a companion
  star ignites on its surface. Most known novae have been seen to erupt once. Some erupt again every few decades:
  recurrent novae. Finding an old eruption on a plate proves a nova recurrent.
- **Hub.** [astr0](https://github.com/Foundation-0/astr0).
- **Sibling project.** [plate-eclipses](https://github.com/Foundation-0/plate-eclipses) uses the same archive (DASCH
  DR7) and the same checks: comparison stars, null tests on constant stars, and re-measurement from the plate images.
  This project reuses its approach.
- **Status.** Pilot, claimed, not peer reviewed. No person outside the project has checked it.
- **Lead.** To be named.

## Checked on 19 Oct 2026

### Against their sources

- Pagnotta and Schaefer, "Identifying and Quantifying Recurrent Novae Masquerading as Classical Novae"
  ([arXiv:1405.0246](https://arxiv.org/abs/1405.0246)): three methods give 24% ± 4%, 12% ± 3% and 35% ± 3%. They
  conclude that roughly a quarter of the 394 known Galactic novae are actually recurrent novae.
- DASCH DR7 ([dasch.cfa.harvard.edu/dr7](https://dasch.cfa.harvard.edu/dr7/)) is the final data release, December
  2024.

### By re-running the pilot

The scouting report described a pilot run of `erupt.py` on five stars. It was re-run on the same light curves on
19 Oct 2026. The output is [results/pilot-2026-10-19.txt](../results/pilot-2026-10-19.txt). Each claim from the
report, with what the re-run found:

| Scouting report | Re-run |
|---|---|
| The rule: at least 3 plates from at least 2 plate series within 60 days, each at least 2 mag above the star's quiet level. | Corrected in detail. `erupt.py` groups measurements more than 2 mag brighter than the median (or a quiet level given on the command line), starting a new group after any gap of more than 60 days, and prints every group with its plate series. It does not apply "3 plates, 2 series". That step was done by hand on its output. Its docstring says it requires 2 plates, but the code does not. |
| Recovered 6 of the 17 known eruptions of T Pyx, U Sco and RS Oph from 1890 to 1989: T Pyx 1902, 1920 and 1944; U Sco 1936 and 1945; RS Oph 1933. | Not reproduced with the script's default. The median gives 5 of 17: U Sco 1936 shows on one plate only (26 Jun 1936, series `rl`). Setting the quiet level of T Pyx and U Sco to their ATLAS catalogue magnitudes (15.55 and 18.21) gives 6 of 17, as reported: U Sco 1936 then has 6 plates from 4 series. |
| Four more show on single plates. | Confirmed in both runs. With the median: U Sco 1917, 1936 and 1979, RS Oph 1898. With the catalogue levels: T Pyx 1890, U Sco 1917 and 1979, RS Oph 1898. |
| Four of the seven misses fall in the Menzel gap. | Four misses fall in 1953–69 in both runs (T Pyx 1967, U Sco 1969, RS Oph 1958 and 1967). There are seven misses with the catalogue levels and eight with the median. |
| No false alarms: on all five stars, every multi-series group belongs to a known eruption or its decline. | Confirmed in both runs. Single plates away from any known eruption do occur, for example T Pyx at magnitude 9.3 in 1936. |
| Relaxed to a single plate series, the rule flags RS Oph from 20 Mar to 18 Apr 1914. Six plates show it at 8.5–9.6 mag against a century median of about 11. | Corrected in detail. The rule flags three plates, 20 to 25 March (series `ai`, 8.45 to 8.74 mag, against a median of 11.24). The light curve shows the star bright on seven plates over six nights from 20 Mar to 18 Apr, at 8.45 to 9.84 mag. The later four are under the 2-mag cut (two plates of 6 Apr) or rejected by DASCH (4 Apr and 18 Apr). |
| Not seen on 13 Feb (fainter than 9.1) or 25 Apr (fainter than 10.5). | Confirmed: plate limits 9.14 and 10.47. |
| Five of the six plates are from one low-resolution series with star images about 2′ across. | Confirmed if counted by night. The seven plates fall on six nights: five with only `ai` plates, and 4 Apr with one `ac` plate. Star images on the `ai` plates are 0.029 to 0.036 degrees across. Also seen: on five of the six `ai` plates the star lies 26 to 28 degrees from the plate centre. |
| DASCH's quality flag rejects two of the six. | Confirmed: `reject_flag` is 5 on 4 Apr and 1 on 18 Apr. The stricter mask of plate-eclipses would also drop the plates of 20 Mar and 6 Apr (both). |

The list of 17 known eruptions used for the scoring (T Pyx 1890, 1902, 1920, 1944, 1967; U Sco 1906, 1917, 1936, 1945,
1969, 1979, 1987; RS Oph 1898, 1933, 1958, 1967, 1985) follows Schaefer 2010
([arXiv:0912.4426](https://arxiv.org/abs/0912.4426)) and has not yet been re-checked against the paper.

T Pyx's light curve came from DASCH's APASS catalogue entry, not from `fetch.sh`, which picks the ATLAS entry and
gets a different light curve of the same star. [pilot/README.md](../pilot/README.md) gives the call for each star.

## From the scouting report, not yet re-checked

- Schaefer lists 402 Galactic novae: "Comprehensive Catalogue of the Overall Best Distances and Properties of 402
  Galactic Novae" ([arXiv:2210.03181](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.03181); table at VizieR
  [J/MNRAS/517/6150](https://vizier.cds.unistra.fr/viz-bin/VizieR?-source=J/MNRAS/517/6150)).
- Harvard took few plates from 1953 to 1969, the "Menzel gap". This hides eruptions in those years. Source: "KIC
  8462852 Faded at an Average Rate of 0.165±0.013 Magnitudes Per Century From 1890 To 1989"
  ([arXiv:1601.03256](https://arxiv.org/abs/1601.03256)).
- Who else: Schaefer, one star at a time. V2487 Oph: "Recurrent Nova V2487 Oph Had Superflares in 1941 and 1942 ..."
  ([arXiv:2405.01210](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.01210)). KT Eri: "The Nova KT Eri Is a Recurrent Nova With a
  Recurrence Time-Scale of 40-50 Years" ([arXiv:2210.10448](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.10448)). An arXiv search for DASCH with novae found only two
  abstracts (2011 and 2020). No systematic sweep of DR7 was found.
- Schaefer infers hidden eruptions of RS Oph in 1907 and 1945: "Comprehensive Photometric Histories of All Known
  Galactic Recurrent Novae" ([arXiv:0912.4426](https://arxiv.org/abs/0912.4426)).
- On the RS Oph 1914 flag: no 1914 eruption appears in the literature the scouting found, and Schaefer has searched
  these plates. The prior is low. It is an unverified flag, not a discovery.

The arXiv links above were opened on 19 Oct 2026 and lead to the papers with the titles given. What the papers say
was not re-checked.
